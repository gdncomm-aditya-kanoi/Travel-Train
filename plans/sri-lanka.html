The short version. Fly in on the morning of Saturday 13 March, land at 12:15 and spend the afternoon in Colombo (Pettah, the red mosque, Galle Face at sunset). On Sunday see a few temples and take the 15:15 train to Habarana, then an Uber to Sigiriya. Then Dambulla and Kandy, the early bus to Nuwara Eliya for a colonial walk and a night in the cool hills, and the 09:00 scenic train from Nanu Oya to Ella with the Nine Arch Bridge at golden hour. In Ella we pick up one tuk-tuk, drive it ourselves to Yala for a safari and then along the south coast, and on the last day we go from Galle Fort to the airport in it, for the 21:50 flight on Sunday 21 March.

4. Yala and the south coast Days 6 to 9

Where is Yala National Park? It is in the south-east of Sri Lanka, in the Hambantota district, on the coast about 300 km from Colombo. The town most jeeps leave from is Tissamaharama, which sits on the way from Ella (hills, about 90 km away) to Mirissa (south coast, about 110 km on). It is a straight line down from Ella to the sea, so it fits between the two. A tuk-tuk cannot go into the park, so we sleep in Tissamaharama and take a jeep. Open Yala in Google Maps. Distances are my estimates, so check them.
Yala costs and times. I found no official fee schedule, and the travel sites disagree, so treat these as ranges and ask the Department of Wildlife Conservation or your guesthouse. Entry: USD 20 for a SAARC adult in one guide, about LKR 9,600 in another, and USD 15 to 25 for other foreigners in a third. One guide adds a vehicle fee of about USD 10 and 18% tax. Jeep, half day: about LKR 12,000 to 25,000 for the whole jeep, which the three of us share, and a tracker or guide is optional at about LKR 2,000 to 4,000. My estimate: about LKR 13,000 to 17,900 each (₹3,650 to ₹5,000) for entry and jeep. Times: there are two entry times, 04:30 in the morning and 14:00 in the afternoon. The plan uses the afternoon entry, with a morning option in the Day 6 timetable. Confirm the gate and last-entry times when you book. March is in the dry season. The park shuts for part of September and October, so March is not affected.

Travel tips

From the 8-day itinerary by a travel creator, which I compared with this plan.

  • Plan around the weather. Sri Lanka has no single best season. Clouds and rain can arrive fast in the hills.
  • Keep some cash. Cards are not taken everywhere.
  • Buy a SIM at the airport exit (Dialog worked for them) or use an eSIM.
  • The climbs add up. Sigiriya and Little Adam's Peak are both steep, so don't stack them in one day.
  • Keep buffer time. A tuk-tuk is slow and a quick stop can take an hour.
  • Sunset is about 18:15 to 18:20 in March (typical, not exact for 2027). Plan to be at The Cliff about 45 minutes before it.
  • Don't try to do everything. Drop a stop and stay longer instead.

DJI Neo 2 drone checklist

Rules first. The sources I found agree that visitors need permission from the Civil Aviation Authority of Sri Lanka (CAASL), and that CAASL grants it per location after the Ministry of Defence confirms the places, which takes several days. They disagree on whether a tourist's drone needs the full import process, so ask CAASL (caa.lk/drones) and Sri Lanka Customs by email and keep the replies. Flying in a national park, over archaeological sites and near airports is not allowed without permission. Rules change, so check them yourself before you go. I have not seen the final 2027 rules.

Before the trip

  • ☐ Email CAASL for tourist flying permission, with the drone model, dates and the places you want to fly. Start 4 to 6 weeks ahead
  • ☐ Ask whether the Ministry of Defence clearance is needed for each place, and keep the approval letters on your phone and on paper
  • ☐ Ask Customs what to declare at the airport, and whether the drone needs a note when you bring it in and out
  • ☐ Update the drone, the controller and the DJI Fly app, and log in once while you still have good internet
  • ☐ Check the airline rule for lithium batteries: they go in the cabin bag, not the checked bag. Note the Wh on each battery
  • ☐ Tape the battery terminals or use a fireproof battery bag, and charge them to about 50% for the flight
  • ☐ Check your travel insurance covers the drone, and take serial numbers and photos of it

Pack

  • ☐ DJI Neo 2, with the propeller guards
  • ☐ All batteries and the charging hub, plus a USB-C cable
  • ☐ Spare propellers and the small screwdriver
  • ☐ Memory card and a card reader, and the controller or goggles if you use them
  • ☐ Phone with the DJI Fly app, and a power bank
  • ☐ Plug adapter. Sri Lanka uses types D, M and G, 230 V
  • ☐ Lens cloth, a small pouch, and a zip bag for sand and salt spray
  • ☐ Printed permission letters and a copy of your passport

When flying

  • ☐ Fly only at approved places, in daylight, and keep the drone in sight
  • ☐ Not in Yala or any other national park. Not at Sigiriya, the Temple of the Sacred Tooth, Galle Fort or other temples and monuments, and not near the airport or any military site
  • ☐ Stay under 120 m, away from crowds, roads and trains, and never over people or private land without asking
  • ☐ Ask the hostel or a local before you launch, and land at once if police or a guard asks
  • ☐ Check wind and rain first. On the coast, take off from a flat clean spot, not loose sand
  • ☐ Charge every battery each night and copy the clips to your phone

Sources: CAASL drone page, drone-laws.com and Sri Lankan news reports. These are secondary and some are dated, so confirm with CAASL.
