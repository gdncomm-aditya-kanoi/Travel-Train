The plan at a glance

The short version. Fly in on the evening of 14 March. Temples and cafés in Colombo, then Sigiriya and Dambulla, then Kandy. From Kandy we drive our own rented tuk-tuk through the hills (Nuwara Eliya, Ella), down to Tissamaharama for a Yala safari, and along the south coast (Mirissa, Ahangama, Galle). We drop the tuk-tuk in Galle, and the coastal train takes us back to Colombo for the last night.

1. Colombo Days 1, 2, 8 and 9

Day 1Sun 14 Mar

Arrive in Colombo

Land at CMB at 20:50, clear immigration and pick up a Dialog or Mobitel tourist SIM. Take the Route 187 airport express bus or a PickMe taxi or tuk-tuk to the central Colombo hostel for the night.

Day 2 (morning)Mon 15 Mar

Temples and cafés

Kelaniya Raja Maha Vihara, Gangaramaya and the floating Seema Malaka on Beira Lake, then the Lotus Tower. Breakfast at Kiri Kōpi on Flower Road and a bowl at Seed. Travel towards Dambulla and Sigiriya in the afternoon or evening.

Day 8 (night)Sun 21 Mar

Back by coastal train

The ocean-side coastal train brings us into Colombo (Mount Lavinia or Colombo Fort) for the last night. Colombo Mount Hostel is right on the train line.

Day 9Mon 22 Mar

Souvenirs and the flight

Pettah Market, which is loud, crowded and very alive, with the red-and-white Jami Ul-Alfar Mosque next to it. Then Ceylon tea, spices and crafts, and a rice and curry lunch. Airport by early evening for the 21:50 flight, landing in Bengaluru at 23:25.

2. The Cultural Triangle Days 2 and 3

Day 2 (evening)Mon 15 Mar

Pidurangala for sunset

If you reach Sigiriya by about 4 PM, climb Pidurangala Rock (about LKR 1,000) for sunset and the best view of Sigiriya itself. It is a second climb, so check the weather first and carry a torch and a rain layer: a creator who did it got caught in a storm and came down in the dark. Skip it if you land late or the sky is black, and do Sigiriya fresh in the morning.

Day 3 (morning)Tue 16 Mar

Sigiriya and Dambulla

Climb Sigiriya Lion Rock early, to beat the midday sun, then visit the Dambulla Royal Cave Temple (five cave shrines and a golden Buddha). Carry the original passport for the SAARC ticket rate, see the price table below.

3. Kandy, Nuwara Eliya and Ella Days 3 to 5

Day 3 (afternoon to night)Tue 16 Mar

Kandy, a full afternoon and evening

On the road in, stop at a wood-carving workshop (the Matale side is known for it) and look at the traditional Sri Lankan masks, which stand for health, happiness, friendship and protection from the evil eye. In Kandy, in this order:

  • Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic (Sri Dalada Maligawa), one of Sri Lanka's most sacred Buddhist sites. Go for the evening puja if you can.
  • Kandy Lake, a slow walk round it, then a café stop. Bubble tea works here.
  • Buddha View Point over Kandy for sunset.
  • The Kandy cultural dance show if you still have energy (from the More places list).

The creator whose plan I compared reached Kandy in the morning and had the whole day there. We can't do that, because Sigiriya comes first. To match them, skip the Dambulla caves, or leave Sigiriya by noon, and you reach Kandy by about 2 PM. Stay by the lake, so the temple, café and walk are all on foot.

Day 4Wed 17 Mar

Kandy to Nuwara Eliya, in our own tuk-tuk

Pick up the tuk-tuk in Kandy in the morning, after you have the permit (see below). The rental company I read lists Kandy as a pick-up town, with a surcharge. Then drive, with the stops in this order:

  1. Ambulawawa Tower, a climbable tower with big views. Skip the top if you dislike heights or small spaces.
  2. Ramboda Falls, for a break, food and the view.
  3. A tea factory tour with a tasting, on the road through the tea estates.
  4. Nuwara Eliya: Lake Gregory in the evening, and a dorm at The Loft.

By my estimate this is about 80 km of steep, bendy road. A tuk-tuk is limited to 40 km/h, and the rental company says it is slower on hills, so allow 4 to 5 hours of driving on top of the stops. Leave Kandy by 8 AM, drop the tower if you are behind, and be in Nuwara Eliya before dark. Don't drive at night, and be careful in rain. Check the distance in Google Maps.

Day 5Thu 18 Mar

Nuwara Eliya to Ella

Leave early. On the way, stop at the Sita Amman Temple (the Ashok Vatika of the Ramayana, with a Hanuman idol and giant footprints) and, if you have the time, Nanu Oya Falls, ideally when a train crosses the bridge. Then drive on to Ella, about 60 km by my estimate. In the afternoon, walk to the Nine Arch Bridge and climb Little Adam's Peak for sunset. Park the tuk-tuk at the hostel and walk around Ella in the evening.

Cheaper versionSkip the Nuwara Eliya night

Train Kandy to Ella, tuk-tuk from Ella

The surcharges for a one-way rental are the biggest cost. This version cuts them. On Day 4 ride the scenic train from Kandy to Ella (reserve a seat, or buy an unreserved ticket at the station for under ₹150), and pick the tuk-tuk up in Ella on Day 5 (surcharge about USD 40, against USD 59 in Kandy). You rent for 4 days, not 5, and the south coast gets a second night. You lose the tower, the falls, the tea factory and Sita Amman. By my sums it saves about ₹1,750 to ₹2,150 each when two people share one tuk-tuk.

The train stops at Nanu Oya, not in Nuwara Eliya itself, so skip the town in this version.

4. Yala and the south coast Days 6 to 8

Day 6Fri 19 Mar

Ella to Yala National Park

Drive down from Ella to Tissamaharama, the town most safari jeeps leave from (about 90 km by my estimate, 3 to 4 hours by tuk-tuk, past Ravana Falls and Ravana Cave if you stop). Sleep in a guesthouse in Tissamaharama, where you can park the tuk-tuk. Then an afternoon jeep safari in Yala National Park. A tuk-tuk cannot go into the park, so you need a jeep and a driver, which your guesthouse can arrange or you can book online. Yala is known for leopards, but sightings are not guaranteed.

What it costs. The SAARC package prices I found are about USD 30 for a half day and USD 65 for a full day, and they cover the park fee and the jeep (see the SAARC table). One reseller lists a private jeep at USD 35 each for five people, and another lists half-day trips from USD 71 each, so the more people in the jeep, the cheaper it is. I could not confirm the official park fees or the safari start times. One operator lists a 5:30 AM departure for a 3-hour morning safari. Ask when you book.

Day 7Sat 20 Mar

Along the coast to Mirissa

Drive from Tissamaharama to Mirissa, about 110 km by my estimate and 4 hours or more, so leave early and stay off the expressway (tuk-tuks are not allowed on highways). Cafés today: Hey Mama, N Ice Cream and Shady Lane in Mirissa, flow café in Weligama. Add Secret Beach and Parrot Rock, and finish at Coconut Tree Hill for sunset, the famous Instagram spot above Mirissa.

Day 8 (morning)Sun 21 Mar

Ahangama cafés and the swing

Marshmellow Surf Café, Cactus and Black Honey in Ahangama, then Dream Cabana for the Dalawella beach swing over the waves.

Day 8 (afternoon)Sun 21 Mar

Galle Fort and the train home

The UNESCO-listed ramparts of Galle Dutch Fort, the Galle Lighthouse and Pedlar Street for cafés and shops, and KiXi Fort Galle. Drop the tuk-tuk in Galle, then take the ocean-side coastal train north to Colombo. The Tsunami Museum at Telwatta is on the way, a serious stop about the December 2004 tsunami, in which more than 30,000 people in Sri Lanka died. Get off there only if you have the time.

Against the ₹50,000 to ₹60,000 target. With the tuk-tuk and Yala, the ground costs are about ₹32,000 to ₹43,200. Add the ₹22,000 for the flights and the total is ₹54,000 to ₹65,200. That is inside the target at the low end and about ₹5,000 over at the high end. The tuk-tuk is what pushed it up: it costs more than the scooters did, mostly because the one-way rental surcharges alone are about USD 109. The cover that removes the USD 150 excess (USD 50) is not in these numbers.
How to bring the tuk-tuk cost down.
  • Take the cheaper version of Day 4 and 5 (the train to Ella, the tuk-tuk from Ella). By my sums that saves about ₹1,750 to ₹2,150 each, and brings the total to about ₹52,250 to ₹63,050.
  • Fill the tuk-tuk. It seats three, so a third person cuts everyone's share by a third. It is the biggest saving.
  • Ask for the surcharge to be waived or reduced for a 5-day booking. The longer you rent, the lower the daily rate.
  • Get the local permit yourself at AA Ceylon (about LKR 3,600, or ₹1,010), not through the company (about USD 40).
  • Ask the local tuk-tuk shops in Kandy and Ella for a quote. I found no prices for them.
  • Share the Yala jeep with other travellers from your guesthouse.
Spend less, eat more. Eat rice and curry or veg kottu at local places for ₹150 to ₹280 and use the cafés for coffee, bowls and dessert, which is about ₹850 a day on food. To drive a tuk-tuk you need your Indian licence, a physical International Driving Permit and a local recognition permit stamped for 3-wheelers (about LKR 3,600, or ₹1,010, from AA Ceylon in Colombo, or arranged by the rental company). Tuk-tuks are limited to 40 km/h, so plan short days. If you take the train to Ella, book reserved 2nd or 3rd class seats early on the official Sri Lanka Railways site, or buy unreserved tickets at the station for under ₹150.

Travel tips

From the 8-day itinerary by a travel creator, which I compared with this plan.

  • Plan around the weather. Sri Lanka has no single best season: the coast you visit sets which months are good. Clouds and rain can arrive fast in the hills.
  • Carry the original Indian passport for the discounted Sigiriya ticket.
  • Keep some cash. Cards are not taken everywhere.
  • Buy a SIM at the airport exit (Dialog worked for them) or use an eSIM for no hassle.
  • The climbs add up. Sigiriya, Pidurangala, Ambulawawa Tower and Little Adam's Peak are all steep. Don't stack them in one day.
  • Keep buffer time. A sunset can turn into a rainstorm and a quick stop can take an hour.
  • Don't try to do everything. Drop a few stops and spend longer in Ella, Nuwara Eliya, Mirissa or Galle.
  • A driver is worth it for a group. It saves a lot of time, and a good one doubles as a guide. The creator paid about US$50 a day.
  • On the flight home, pick a seat on the other side of the aircraft if you want to spot Rama Setu again. I don't know which side that is.

Check these before booking

  • Two versions of the plan. I also had an earlier version for 28 Feb to 8 Mar with no Sigiriya or Dambulla. This page is the newer one.
  • The totals do not match. The nine daily totals in the old table added up to about ₹19,850 to ₹28,700, while the category table said ₹23,350 to ₹34,200 before the tuk-tuk and Yala. The category table also counts the visa, SIM and buffer, so plan for the higher range.
  • The night count. Colombo 2 (nights 1 and 8), Sigiriya or Dambulla 1, Kandy 1, Nuwara Eliya 1, Ella 1, Tissamaharama 1 and the south coast 1 makes 8. The south coast now gets one night, not three, to fit the hills and Yala. To give it more, take the cheaper version of Days 4 and 5.
  • Nuwara Eliya is not on the rail line. The Kandy to Ella train stops at Nanu Oya, a bus or tuk-tuk ride from the town.
  • Tuk-tuk permits. Without your licence, a physical International Driving Permit and the local recognition permit stamped for 3-wheelers, you cannot rent. Check that your Indian licence class covers a three-wheeler, and ask the rental company in writing about the one-way surcharge, the insurance excess and the drop-off in Galle.
  • My distances are estimates. The 80, 60, 90 and 110 km legs and the driving times are my own guesses at tuk-tuk speed. Check each leg in Google Maps before you book the nights.
