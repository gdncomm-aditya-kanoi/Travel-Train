The plan at a glance

The short version. Fly in on the evening of 14 March. Temples and cafés in Colombo, then Sigiriya and Dambulla, Kandy, the scenic train to Ella, and four days on the south coast by scooter (Mirissa, Weligama, Ahangama, Galle). The coastal train takes us back to Colombo for the last night.

1. Colombo Days 1, 2, 8 and 9

Day 1Sun 14 Mar

Arrive in Colombo

Land at CMB at 20:50, clear immigration and pick up a Dialog or Mobitel tourist SIM. Take the Route 187 airport express bus or a PickMe taxi or tuk-tuk to the central Colombo hostel for the night.

Day 2 (morning)Mon 15 Mar

Temples and cafés

Kelaniya Raja Maha Vihara, Gangaramaya and the floating Seema Malaka on Beira Lake, then the Lotus Tower. Breakfast at Kiri Kōpi on Flower Road and a bowl at Seed. Travel towards Dambulla and Sigiriya in the afternoon or evening.

Day 8 (night)Sun 21 Mar

Back by coastal train

The ocean-side coastal train brings us into Colombo (Mount Lavinia or Colombo Fort) for the last night. Colombo Mount Hostel is right on the train line.

Day 9Mon 22 Mar

Souvenirs and the flight

Pettah Market, which is loud, crowded and very alive, with the red-and-white Jami Ul-Alfar Mosque next to it. Then Ceylon tea, spices and crafts, and a rice and curry lunch. Airport by early evening for the 21:50 flight, landing in Bengaluru at 23:25.

Cafés in Colombo

2. The Cultural Triangle Days 2 and 3

Day 2 (evening)Mon 15 Mar

Pidurangala for sunset

If you reach Sigiriya by about 4 PM, climb Pidurangala Rock (about LKR 1,000) for sunset and the best view of Sigiriya itself. It is a second climb, so check the weather first and carry a torch and a rain layer: a creator who did it got caught in a storm and came down in the dark. Skip it if you land late or the sky is black, and do Sigiriya fresh in the morning.

Day 3 (morning)Tue 16 Mar

Sigiriya and Dambulla

Climb Sigiriya Lion Rock early, to beat the midday sun, then visit the Dambulla Royal Cave Temple (five cave shrines and a golden Buddha). Carry the original passport for the SAARC ticket rate, see the price table below.

3. Kandy, Nuwara Eliya and Ella Days 3 and 4

Day 3 (afternoon to night)Tue 16 Mar

Kandy, a full afternoon and evening

On the road in, stop at a wood-carving workshop (the Matale side is known for it) and look at the traditional Sri Lankan masks, which stand for health, happiness, friendship and protection from the evil eye. In Kandy, in this order:

  • Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic (Sri Dalada Maligawa), one of Sri Lanka's most sacred Buddhist sites. Go for the evening puja if you can.
  • Kandy Lake, a slow walk round it, then a café stop. Bubble tea works here.
  • Buddha View Point over Kandy for sunset.
  • The Kandy cultural dance show if you still have energy (from the More places list).

The creator whose plan I compared reached Kandy in the morning and had the whole day there. We can't do that, because Sigiriya comes first. To match them, skip the Dambulla caves, or leave Sigiriya by noon, and you reach Kandy by about 2 PM. Stay by the lake, so the temple, café and walk are all on foot.

Day 4Wed 17 Mar

The mountain train to Ella

Ride the scenic train through the tea estates, taking in Nuwara Eliya (the Tudor cottages, Gregory Lake and the tea plantations) on the way to Ella. In Ella, rent a scooter or scooty for the Nine Arch Bridge and Little Adam's Peak.

The train stops at Nanu Oya, not in Nuwara Eliya itself, so the town needs a bus or tuk-tuk ride and some extra time.

Day 4 (extras)Wed 17 Mar

Stops along the way

The creator drove this stretch, so they could stop at Ambulawawa Tower (a climbable tower, only for people happy with heights), Ramboda Falls, a tea factory tour with a tasting, Nanu Oya Falls (time it with a train crossing the bridge), Lake Gregory and the Sita Amman Temple, the Ashok Vatika of the Ramayana. By train we can fit the tea factory, Gregory Lake and Sita Amman at most, and only if we leave Kandy early. A rented tuk-tuk or a shared car from Nanu Oya covers them.

Their other train option was the Calypso train from Bandarawela to Ella, with about 15 minutes at the Nine Arch Bridge for photos. I could not check that it still runs, so confirm it at the station before you plan around it.

4. The south coast Days 5 to 8

Day 5Thu 18 Mar

Down to the coast

Take a morning bus or a shared transfer from Ella to the south coast, based in Mirissa or Ahangama, and rent a scooter for the coast road. Cafés today: Hey Mama and N Ice Cream in Mirissa. If you are up early, Little Adam's Peak at sunrise first. The road down passes Ravana Falls, and the Ravana Cave is close by: ask the bus to drop you there, or stop on the way by tuk-tuk.

Day 6Fri 19 Mar

The café trail

Shady Lane in Mirissa, flow café in Weligama, then Marshmellow Surf Café, Cactus and Black Honey in Ahangama, all by scooter. Add Secret Beach, Parrot Rock and, for sunset, Coconut Tree Hill, which is the famous Instagram spot above Mirissa.

Day 7Sat 20 Mar

The swing and Galle Fort

Dream Cabana for the Dalawella beach swing over the waves, then the UNESCO-listed ramparts of Galle Dutch Fort, the Galle Lighthouse and Pedlar Street for cafés and shops, and KiXi Fort Galle. Allow half a day or more: the fort is worth the time.

Day 8 (morning)Sun 21 Mar

A last swim

A final morning swim, return the scooter, and in the afternoon take the ocean-side coastal train north to Colombo. The Tsunami Museum at Telwatta, about halfway to Colombo, is a serious stop about the December 2004 tsunami, in which more than 30,000 people in Sri Lanka died. Get off there only if you have the time, and check whether the museum is open and how to get from the station.

Against the ₹50,000 to ₹60,000 target. The ground costs above are ₹23,350 to ₹34,200. That leaves about ₹15,800 to ₹36,650 for the two IndiGo flights, so check the fare you actually book. The extra stops I added are mostly free or a few hundred rupees (Pidurangala is about ₹281). I found no price for Ambulawawa Tower or a tea factory tour, so check those. If the flights come in cheap, the spare money pays for a hired car for the Kandy to Nuwara Eliya day, which is how the creator did it, or a safari.
Spend less, eat more. Eat rice and curry or veg kottu at local places for ₹150 to ₹280 and use the cafés for coffee, bowls and dessert, which is about ₹850 a day on food. To ride a scooter you need an International Driving Permit with a two-wheeler endorsement and a local recognition permit (about LKR 3,000 to 5,000, or ₹850 to ₹1,400, from AA Ceylon in Colombo or your rental agency). For the Kandy to Ella train, book reserved 2nd or 3rd class seats early on the official Sri Lanka Railways site, or buy unreserved tickets at the station for under ₹150.

SAARC entry prices

India is in SAARC, so many sites in Sri Lanka charge us a lower rate than other foreigners, but only with the original passport. Photocopies and photos are not accepted at the ticket counters. Prices change and the sources disagree, so treat every number as an estimate and check the Central Cultural Fund e-ticketing site (eservices.ccf.gov.lk) before you go. Travel tips

From the 8-day itinerary by a travel creator, which I compared with this plan.

  • Plan around the weather. Sri Lanka has no single best season: the coast you visit sets which months are good. Clouds and rain can arrive fast in the hills.
  • Carry the original Indian passport for the discounted Sigiriya ticket.
  • Keep some cash. Cards are not taken everywhere.
  • Buy a SIM at the airport exit (Dialog worked for them) or use an eSIM for no hassle.
  • The climbs add up. Sigiriya, Pidurangala, Ambulawawa Tower and Little Adam's Peak are all steep. Don't stack them in one day.
  • Keep buffer time. A sunset can turn into a rainstorm and a quick stop can take an hour.
  • Don't try to do everything. Drop a few stops and spend longer in Ella, Nuwara Eliya, Mirissa or Galle.
  • A driver is worth it for a group. It saves a lot of time, and a good one doubles as a guide. The creator paid about US$50 a day.
  • On the flight home, pick a seat on the other side of the aircraft if you want to spot Rama Setu again. I don't know which side that is.

Check these before booking

  • Two versions of the plan. I also had an earlier version for 28 Feb to 8 Mar with no Sigiriya or Dambulla. This page is the newer one.
  • The totals do not match. The nine daily totals add up to about ₹19,850 to ₹28,700, while the category table says ₹23,350 to ₹34,200. The category table also counts the visa, SIM and buffer, so plan for the higher range.
  • The night count. The budget table says 4 nights on the south coast and 2 in the middle. Counting the days above gives Colombo 2, Sigiriya or Dambulla 1, Kandy 1, Ella 1 and the south coast 3, which is also 8.
  • Nuwara Eliya is not on the rail line. The Kandy to Ella train stops at Nanu Oya, a bus or tuk-tuk ride from the town.
  • Scooter permits. Without an International Driving Permit and the local recognition permit, the scooter days do not work.
