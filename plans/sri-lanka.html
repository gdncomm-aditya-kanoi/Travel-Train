The plan at a glance

The short version. Fly in on the evening of 14 March. Temples and cafés in Colombo, then Sigiriya and Dambulla, Kandy and the scenic train to Ella. In Ella we pick up one tuk-tuk, drive it ourselves to Yala for a safari and then along the south coast, and drop it in Galle. The coastal train takes us back to Colombo for the last night.

This map is from my first planning PDF. The route now skips a stop in Nuwara Eliya (the train passes it) and adds Yala, between Ella and Mirissa.

4. Yala and the south coast Days 5 to 8

Where is Yala National Park? It is in the south-east of Sri Lanka, in the Hambantota district, on the coast about 300 km from Colombo. The town most jeeps leave from is Tissamaharama, which sits on the way from Ella (hills, about 90 km away) to Mirissa (south coast, about 110 km on). It is a straight line down from Ella to the sea, so it fits between the two. A tuk-tuk cannot go into the park, so we sleep in Tissamaharama and take a jeep. Open Yala in Google Maps. Distances are my estimates, so check them.
Yala costs and times. I found no official fee schedule, and the travel sites disagree, so treat these as ranges and ask the Department of Wildlife Conservation or your guesthouse. Entry: USD 20 for a SAARC adult in one guide, about LKR 9,600 in another, and USD 15 to 25 for other foreigners in a third. One guide adds a vehicle fee of about USD 10 and 18% tax. Jeep, half day: about LKR 12,000 to 25,000 for the whole jeep, which the three of us share, and a tracker or guide is optional at about LKR 2,000 to 4,000. My estimate: about LKR 13,000 to 17,900 each (₹3,650 to ₹5,000) for entry and jeep. Times: the park is open about 6 AM to 6 PM, tickets are sold until about 4 PM, and one guide lists the morning drive as 6 to 10 AM and the afternoon drive as 2 to 6 PM. March is in the dry season. The park shuts for part of September and October, so March is not affected.

Where to book it

The websites I would use, grouped by what you need. "Official" means the government or the operator itself. "Reseller" sites charge more. Prices and rules change, so check each site before you pay.

SAARC entry prices

Travel tips

From the 8-day itinerary by a travel creator, which I compared with this plan.

  • Plan around the weather. Sri Lanka has no single best season. Clouds and rain can arrive fast in the hills.
  • Keep some cash. Cards are not taken everywhere.
  • Buy a SIM at the airport exit (Dialog worked for them) or use an eSIM.
  • The climbs add up. Sigiriya and Little Adam's Peak are both steep, so don't stack them in one day.
  • Keep buffer time. A tuk-tuk is slow and a quick stop can take an hour.
  • Don't try to do everything. Drop a stop and stay longer instead.

Check these before booking

  • The times and distances are my estimates. The 90 and 110 km tuk-tuk legs, the train and bus times and the safari slot all need checking in Google Maps and with the operators.
  • Tuk-tuk paperwork. Without your licence, a physical International Driving Permit and the local permit stamped for 3-wheelers, you cannot rent. Check that your Indian licence class covers a three-wheeler, and get the one-way surcharge, the insurance excess and the Galle drop-off in writing.
  • Three people in one tuk-tuk. Confirm that the model and the bags fit.
  • Yala. The fees and jeep prices come from travel sites that disagree, and I found no official schedule, so ask the guesthouse or the wildlife department before you book.
  • Rooms. No price on the room list is for March 2027, so check each one for your dates.
