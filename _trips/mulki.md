Mulki is a tiny, laid-back surf village near Mangalore, where mornings start with waves and evenings end with good food and new friends. I went for my first surfing experience with three friends on a 4-day coast trip. The trip is split into three pages: this one for the surfing and the stay, [Mangalore]({{ '/trips/mangalore/' | relative_url }}) for the city day, and [Udupi]({{ '/trips/udupi/' | relative_url }}) for the afternoon on the Udupi side.

### Getting there

We took a night train from Bengaluru on 6 March and reached Mulki by the morning of 7 March. The ticket worked out to ₹366 each after cancellations and extras. The station sign is the first thing you see, and the beach is a short way from there.

### Where to stay

We stayed with the surf school, a stay and surfing package that cost ₹7,500 for the four of us, so ₹1,500 each. The stay sits by the backwaters, with a riverside deck for slow mornings, tents and hammocks for the evenings, and a couple of cats and a dog around. I did not note the exact name or address of the hostel, so check the school's Instagram, @mulki.in, for the latest.

### The days

**Day 1: arrive and go to Mangalore.** We reached in the morning, freshened up, had brunch and took the bus to Mangalore to rent a bike and explore. We missed the first surf session because of this, so we planned two sessions the next day. The full Mangalore day is on the [Mangalore page]({{ '/trips/mangalore/' | relative_url }}), and we came back to Mulki late at night.

**Day 2: two sessions in the morning, then Udupi.** This is the surf day. Two sessions, one after the other, with a breakfast of bread omelette and filter coffee after the surf. We also took an ice bath in a big tub after the session (they forced me to jump in first). After that we got ready and went to Udupi: see the [Udupi page]({{ '/trips/udupi/' | relative_url }}).

**Day 3: the last session, then home.** One more session, then checkout and a wander around Mulki. Evenings here mean sunset on the sand by the tents, and the mornings can mean sunrise kayaking on the backwaters. We left by 4 PM for Mangalore, returned the bike by 6 PM and took a local auto to the station for the train back to Bengaluru. On Day 4 we reached Bengaluru and took a cab home.

### What to pack

The surf school provides the board. You bring the rest. This is my list, and what I carried myself:

- A pair of clothes, and swimwear for surfing
- A rash guard or surf tee, quick-dry shorts and a surf hat
- Sunscreen, a water bottle and slippers
- A towel, a bedsheet or shawl for the train, and a pair of shoes
- Driving licence and Aadhar card (you need the licence to rent the bike in Mangalore)
- A cap, toothbrush and toothpaste, skincare, and some emergency tablets

### What I'd add to this plan

<div class="callout"><strong>A few things worth adding</strong>, from reading around rather than from my own trip, so check them locally:
<ul>
<li><strong>Book early.</strong> A stay with surfing fills up on weekends, so confirm the dates before you buy the train ticket.</li>
<li><strong>Go with the instructor's advice on the water.</strong> Ask about the current and the tide before you go in.</li>
<li><strong>Re-apply sunscreen.</strong> The sun on the water is stronger than it feels, so use a rash guard.</li>
<li><strong>Keep your phone dry.</strong> Leave it at the stay or in a waterproof pouch during sessions.</li>
<li><strong>Carry cash.</strong> Small shops and autos may not take UPI.</li>
</ul></div>
