Fort Kochi is the old Portuguese, Dutch and British quarter at the tip of Kochi, a few streets of white churches, painted gates, galleries and cafés beside the sea. I went with a friend for three days on a rented bike, with Mattancherry, Cherai and Ernakulam as the side trips, and all the costs below are per person.

<div class="callout"><strong>The short version.</strong> Night bus in, a Zostel in Fort Kochi, a bike for three days, cafés for most meals, the fishing nets at sunset, a ferry to Vypin and Cherai on Day 2, and Jew Town and the Navy museum on the way home on Day 3.</div>

### Getting there and getting around

We took an overnight bus from Bengaluru, with a chai stop in Tamil Nadu on the way, and reached Kochi in the morning. The bus home left from Vytila Junction in Ernakulam, so on the last day we took an auto there. In Kochi we rented a bike from Royal Brothers (an auto to the shop first), and filled it up three times. For the water we used the Water Metro and the Vypin ferry, which cost very little.

### Where to stay

Zostel Kochi, in Fort Kochi, which was ₹2,936 for two. The café at the Zostel did our dinner on Day 2 and breakfast on Day 3, and it sits in the middle of the lanes, so you can walk to most of the cafés.

### Day 1: arrive, bike and cafés

After checking in we took an auto to the bike shop, filled the tank, and went café to café: a coffee at Kashi Art Café, a sandwich at Arasta, and chai with a samosa at Chez Teapot. We also took the Water Metro out and back, saw the Chinese fishing nets, and had dinner at Bhat's Restaurant.

### Day 2: Mattancherry, Vypin and Cherai

We started at Lila Art Café, then rode to Mattancherry Palace (a tiny entry fee, and the old murals inside are the reason to go, from reading around). From the Fort Kochi jetty we took the ferry to Vypin and rode up to Cherai beach for lunch at Chilli Out Café, right on the sand. Then back on the ferry, a refuel, and dinner at the Zostel café.

### Day 3: Jew Town, Ernakulam and home

Breakfast at the Zostel café, then Ginger House for lunch by the water in Jew Town and Mocha Art Café for coffee. This is where I bought spices that felt like a scam, so compare before you buy. The rest of the day, roughly in this order: snacks at Cochin Bakery, a cloakroom for the bags, the Vypin to Fort Kochi ferry, the Navy museum, a coconut ice cream in Ernakulam, an auto to Vytila Junction and the bus home.

### Curated heritage walks

Fort Kochi has three marked heritage walks that all start at Fort Kochi Beach and pass the old lighthouse. These are the maps and route strips for each, with the numbered landmarks. The maps and strips are from a Fort Kochi heritage-walk guide and are not mine, and I do not have its source or licence.

**Cultural Promenade.** The long one, about 2 km along the waterfront from Fort Kochi Beach to the boat jetty for Ernakulam: the old lighthouse, State Bank of India, Thakur House, Cochin Club, Bastion Bungalow, Tower House, Jawahar Nehru Park, the Chinese fishing nets, the Water Metro terminal, Brunton Boatyard, Folklore Cultural Theatre, Aspinwall, Calvathy Mosque, Fort House Hotel, Hotel Seagull and Pepper House.

### What I'd add to this plan

<div class="callout"><strong>A few things worth adding</strong>, from reading around rather than from my own trip, so check them locally:
<ul>
<li><strong>Be at the nets for the evening.</strong> The fishing nets are worked in the morning and evening, and are quieter than you expect.</li>
<li><strong>Check the Mattancherry Palace and synagogue timings.</strong> Both have a closed day and a lunch break.</li>
<li><strong>Ferry timings change.</strong> Ask at the jetty for the last boat back from Vypin before you ride to Cherai.</li>
<li><strong>Compare spice prices</strong> in two or three shops before you buy anything.</li>
</ul></div>
