Thanjavur (Tanjore to most of us) is where the Cholas put their best stone work, and it sits in the middle of the Cauvery delta, so a bike can reach a lot in a day. I went with a friend for two days: the first around Thanjavur and out to the Kallanai dam, the second on a bike to Kumbakonam and Gangaikonda Cholapuram. I had few notes, so I filled the gaps from a tourist brochure, and I say so where I did.

<div class="callout"><strong>The short version.</strong> Overnight train in, a guest house, the Big Temple, the palace and the Kallanai dam on Day 1, a rented bike on Day 2 for Kumbakonam and Gangaikonda Cholapuram, then a night bus home.</div>

### Getting there

I took the overnight train from Bengaluru to Thanjavur (₹780 for two, so ₹390 each) and the bus back (₹1,838 for two, ₹919 each). Thanjavur Junction is right in the city, and Tiruchirappalli is the nearest airport, about 61 km away. Getting to the Bengaluru station and back home on the metro and buses added a little more.

### Where to stay

ABM Guest House, ₹1,500 for the room, so ₹750 each. It was a simple, cheap base and I did not need more.

### Getting around

In town I used autos (₹200 each time, split between us). I hired a bike for ₹800 and filled it twice (₹420 and ₹340). The bike shop I used and the stretch from our stay are in the box below.

### Day 1: Thanjavur and the Kallanai dam

Start with the **Brihadeeswarar Temple**, called the Big Temple. Raja Raja Chola built it in the 10th century, and UNESCO lists it as a World Heritage Site. The tower over the sanctum is 64.8 m tall. Inside there is a huge lingam, and a Nandi cut from a single rock outside, the second largest in India. Look at the inner wall of the first floor: 108 dance poses are carved there. Most temples have a big gateway and a small tower, but here the tower is the big thing and the gateway is small.

Walk next door to **Sivaganga Park**, a large tank with long walls that is known for its sweet water. Then go on to the **Thanjavur Maratha Palace**, built partly by the Nayaks and partly by the Marathas. It is a big, airy complex with tall halls, painted ceilings and carved pillars, and several things to see in one place: the Durbar Hall, an art gallery of bronzes and stone sculptures, the Saraswathi Mahal Library and the Royal Museum. The palace tickets cost ₹20 and ₹100 for the two of us. Do not skip the Durbar Hall.

Then go out to **Kallanai (the Grand Anicut)**, about 20 km from the city. Karikala Chola built it in the 2nd century to guide the Cauvery into the delta, and it still works. Go for dusk and watch the water rush over the weir.

Save the night for the Big Temple again. Lit up, it is a different place.

### The dancing dolls

Thanjavur is famous for its dancing dolls, clay figures that nod and sway on a round base, and for its gold-leaf paintings. I bought a doll and a fridge magnet (₹650 for both) at one of the shops near the palace. Look around before you buy, since the prices and the quality swing a lot.

### Day 2: Kumbakonam and Gangaikonda Cholapuram

Pick up the bike early, since this is a long ride. I did Kumbakonam first and Cholapuram after it, and then rode back for the night bus.

- **Kumbakonam**, about 40 km from Thanjavur. A temple town, with big old temples such as Sarangapani, Kumbeswarar, Nageswara and Ramaswamy. It is known for the Mahamaham festival, which comes round once in 12 years.
- **Gangaikonda Cholapuram.** Rajendra Chola built this one around 1035 after his campaigns, as the temple of his new capital. It is quieter than Thanjavur, with lawns, a calm courtyard and fine carvings on the walls. It is also a World Heritage Site.

Bring water and snacks, keep the petrol in mind (I filled up twice), and give yourself time for the ride back.

### Food

Breakfast was Coffee Palace both mornings, for good coffee and rava dosa (₹250 and ₹200 for two). For dinner I liked Sri Krishna Bhavan, where the dosa is the thing to order, and for a proper lunch Chellammal's does a buffet in clay pots. The little shops near the new bus stand sell eggs and Kerala parotta cheaply. All of them are on the list on the right, with maps.

### What I'd add to this plan

<div class="callout"><strong>A few things worth adding</strong>, from reading around rather than from my own trip, so check them locally:
<ul>
<li><strong>Darasuram.</strong> The Airavatesvara Temple is on the way between Thanjavur and Kumbakonam and is known for its small, fine sculptures.</li>
<li><strong>Cover up and take off your shoes.</strong> These are working temples, so wear clothes that cover your shoulders and knees. Shoe keeping costs a few rupees.</li>
<li><strong>Visit in winter.</strong> The delta is hot and humid for most of the year, and Oct to Mar is the comfortable window.</li>
<li><strong>Check the Wednesday closures.</strong> The museum and library close that day, so plan the palace around it.</li>
<li><strong>Carry cash.</strong> Small shops, autos and parking mostly take cash.</li>
<li><strong>Pace the bike day.</strong> Kumbakonam and Cholapuram are about 80 km apart, so start early. Darasuram fits in on the way.</li>
</ul></div>
